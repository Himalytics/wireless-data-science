# Uplift ranking and policy evaluation

**Named technique:** T-learner uplift ranking with held-out inverse-propensity policy evaluation. Task: rank released Criteo users by the difference between two predicted visit probabilities, then compare batch assignment at a hypothetical capacity. Scientific practices: independent grouped train/selection/test boundaries, randomized-source identification audit, paired profile-cluster uncertainty, treatment-balance/overlap diagnostics and estimator/capacity sensitivity.

Two separate outcome classifiers are trained on each assignment arm. Candidates: standardized logistic regressions with C=0.1 or 1; histogram gradient boosting with 7 or 15 leaves, 100 iterations, minimum leaf 100, L2=10, no internal early stopping. The deliberately small four-candidate search limits selection noise. All preprocessing uses training rows only. Four candidate pairs (8 fits), plus one logistic assignment audit fit. Validation selects the highest top-20% IPW contrast; there is no test tuning or train+validation refit. A model is not selected separately at each reported capacity. All diagnostics and decile comparisons on test are exploratory, not further model-selection inputs.

Baselines: no assignment, expected random 20% assignment, treat all, and treated-response logistic ranking at the same 20% capacity. Response ranking uses the C=1 treated-arm logistic model; its probability scores are predictive, not uplift. Random allocation is analytically integrated (constant policy weight), not one lucky simulated assignment. Capacity is hypothetical; there is no observed budget, cost or revenue.

For assignment T, visit Y and training assignment share p, the IPW contribution is `T*Y/p - (1-T)*Y/(1-p)`. Policy contrast equals its average times the allocation indicator; multiply by 1,000 for visits per 1,000 eligible users. The denominator includes unassigned users. This is incremental contrast relative to assigning nobody under the working identification assumptions, not the observed response rate of selected users. Primary comparisons subtract random-20% and response-20% policy contributions *row by row* before uncertainty estimation. No independent-fold t-test is used.

Conditional intervals use 500 Poisson(1) profile-cluster multiplier replicates, recomputing the weighted numerator and denominator. Policies, sample splits, model fits, batch ranking and training propensity remain fixed. Intervals address held-out sampling variation at observed feature-profile clusters; they omit fitting, selection, propensity, privacy-selection and unobserved experiment-cluster uncertainty. They are pointwise, not simultaneous. The batch top-k rule uses covariates only and source-order ties; uncertainty is conditional on the observed allocation, rather than a refitted future-batch policy. The model does not provide calibrated individual effect intervals.

Sensitivity: DR scores with fixed training-only 7-leaf outcome models; approximate published p=0.85; training-only logistic covariate propensity clipped to [0.05,0.95]; alternative capacities. DR adds outcome residual corrections to the predicted effect. Agreement is reassuring about estimator choice, not a solution to unknown privacy selection. The propensity model is a diagnostic, not a recovered original randomization mechanism.

No serialized estimator is required: reproduction fits all models. See `results/selection.csv`, `experiment.json` and findings for actual selected model and metrics. Production use is unsupported: no identifiable telecom population, no economic objective, no temporal replication and no verified treatment-cost or retention evidence. A wireless application requires a separately governed, properly randomized offer experiment and corresponding customer outcomes.
