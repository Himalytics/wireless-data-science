## Observational unit, assignment and outcomes

Publisher describes one row per user drawn from several randomized advertising incrementality tests. `treatment` identifies advertising eligibility/assignment (not confirmed exposure). `visit` is a binary observed website visit; `conversion` is another outcome; `exposure` records realized ad exposure. Only twelve anonymous randomly projected features `f0`–`f11` are eligible baseline predictors. Their physical meaning and measurement times cannot independently be audited. Exclude exposure and conversion entirely from predictors; conditioning on compliance can destroy assignment identification. There are no authentic event timestamps, experiment IDs, subscriber identifiers or pre-period outcomes. No calendar or temporal validation is manufactured. Published observation-window timing is not independently recoverable from the CSV; do not infer it from source row order.

## Why version and estimand matter

Version 1 had advertiser-dependent incrementality leakage. The official erratum identifies corrected v2.1; row count and checksum enforce it. Separately, the publisher says nonuniform privacy subsampling prevents recovering original incrementality. Pooling tests and unknown privacy selection mean randomization in the original trials alone does not identify original-population effects in the released sample. A near-chance treatment-prediction AUC is a diagnostic, not proof of exchangeability or known assignment propensity.

We report an **inverse-propensity weighted released-benchmark visit contrast** under a constant-assignment working model. It is useful for comparing benchmark ranking methods; causal interpretation within the released sample additionally needs selection-compatible exchangeability, consistency, no interference and positivity. These assumptions cannot be fully checked. Results do not estimate individual causal truth, original campaign ROI, wireless retention or employer impact. The study does not claim a U.S. carrier result.

## Sampling and integrity

Scan every source row in 250,000-row chunks; retain rows for which SplitMix64(zero-based ordinal) modulo 20 equals zero. This outcome-blind deterministic approximately 5% sample is reproducible, not a leading-file convenience subset. Source order is used only as a row identifier. Hash identical float64 baseline profiles together; split groups approximately 50/20/30 into train/validation/test using the protocol. Profile grouping is conservative: collisions or genuinely distinct users with identical anonymized profiles may be grouped. It cannot recover unknown person/experiment dependencies. All full-row and feature-profile duplicates in the sample are audited. Never treat duplicates as verified repeated people.

Binary/schema/finite-value checks are hard gates. `results/audit.csv` gives per-arm support and observed outcome rates for all splits; `balance.csv` gives train standardized mean differences. `splits.csv.gz` preserves sampled source ordinals, profile hashes and assignments. `predictions.csv.gz` preserves holdout outcomes, model scores and evaluation quantities; both are adapted data under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. No proprietary or employer observations are used. Source dates, source populations and customer identities are never fabricated or joined to telecom data.
